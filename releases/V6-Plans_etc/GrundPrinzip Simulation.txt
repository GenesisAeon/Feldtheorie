## 1. SPECIOUS PRESENT & PSYCHOLOGICAL MOMENT

### 1.1 Historical Context

The concept of the "specious present" was introduced by **E.R. Clay (1882)** and developed by **William James (1890)**, who defined it as *"the prototype of all conceived times... the short duration of which we are immediately and incessantly sensible."*

### 1.2 Empirical Temporal Scales

**Fraisse (1984)** established three fundamental "orders of duration":
- **(a) < 100 ms:** Perception as **instantaneity** (no temporal extension)
- **(b) 100 ms – 5 sec:** Perception of **duration in the perceived present**
- **(c) > 5 sec:** Estimations involving **memory**

**Critical insight:** The lower bound of 100ms marks the transition from instantaneous to temporally extended perception.

### 1.3 Contemporary Estimates

Multiple independent lines of evidence converge on similar timescales:

| Source | Duration | Method | Context |
|--------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Strawson (2009)** | ~300 ms | Philosophical analysis | Direct apprehension |
| **Benussi (early 20th c.)** | 750 ms | Tachistoscopic experiments | Graz school |
| **Pöppel (various)** | 2-3 sec | Ambiguous figure switching | Too long for direct perception |
| **Picard & Craig (2009)** | 125 ms | Neural microstates | "Atoms of thought" |
| **Dainton (2000)** | ≤500 ms | Theoretical | Conservative estimate |
| **Lockwood (2005)** | 1-1.5 sec | Theoretical | Liberal estimate |

### 1.4 Elementary Psychological Moment

**Brecher & contemporaries** identified a finer-grained **elementary temporal window of 50-60ms**, interpreted as:
- Neural synchronization window
- Snapshot of perceptual event structure
- **NOT** the same as the full specious present

### 1.5 Multisensory Integration

**Critical finding:** Audiovisual asynchrony detection requires **100-200ms** integration window (Vatakis & Spence 2007, van Wassenhove 2009).

**Brain synchronization requirement:** Different sensory modalities process at different speeds. Vision requires ~100ms "waiting time" to synchronize with other senses (Eagleman "Brain Time").

### 1.6 Speech Processing Hierarchy

**Poeppel (2003)** identified dual temporal integration windows:
- **Left hemisphere:** 20-60ms (phoneme length)
- **Right hemisphere:** 100-300ms (syllable length)

This suggests **hierarchical temporal organization** with the 100-300ms window representing higher-order integration.

### 1.7 Visual Awareness Threshold

**fMRI studies:** 100% reliability in visual awareness requires stimulus presentations ≥**150ms** (Deary et al. 2004, Kranczioch et al. 2005).

## 2. STRUCTURE FROM MOTION (SfM) REQUIREMENTS

### 2.1 Fundamental Mathematical Requirements

**Minimum constraints for 3D reconstruction:**

1. **Epipolar Geometry:** 2 views minimum (Fundamental Matrix, 8-point algorithm)
2. **Perspective-n-Point (PnP):** n ≥ 3 points for camera pose estimation
3. **Triangulation:** 2+ views for 3D point reconstruction

### 2.2 Practical Implementation

**Tomasi-Kanade Algorithm (1992):**
- Assumes orthographic projection (simplified)
- Input: keypoint trajectories over time
- Output: 3D structure + camera motion
- Extended to perspective projection (Sturm & Triggs)

**Critical insight:** Even simplified models require **temporal sequences**, not single snapshots.

### 2.3 Scale Ambiguity Problem

**Fundamental limitation:** Monocular SfM can only recover structure **up to scale**.

Recovery requires either:
- Known object size in scene
- Known baseline distance
- Multiple views with sufficient parallax

### 2.4 Feature Tracking vs. Matching

| Method | Use Case | Constraint |
|--------|----------|------------|
| **KLT (Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi)** | Close camera positions | Temporal continuity |
| **SIFT Feature Matching** | Wide baselines | Robust to viewpoint change |

### 2.5 Noise & Robustness

**RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus):** Essential for outlier rejection in real-world conditions.

**Observation:** More frames → better noise averaging → more robust reconstruction

### 2.6 Bundle Adjustment

**Final optimization step:** Simultaneously refine:
- Camera poses
- 3D point locations
- Minimize reprojection error

**Computational cost:** ~hour for 8-10 images (traditional methods)

### 2.7 Implications for Biological Vision

**Key insight:** While mathematical minimum is 2 frames, **robust biological 3D perception** likely requires:
- Multiple frames for noise averaging
- Temporal integration window
- Sufficient parallax accumulation
- Motion signal disambiguation

## 5. MULTI-OBJECTIVE OPTIMIZATION IN BIOLOGY

### 5.1 Pareto Optimality Framework

**Core concept:** When multiple objectives conflict, no single "best" solution exists. Instead:
- **Pareto-optimal set:** Solutions where improving one objective worsens another
- **Pareto front:** Boundary of optimal trade-offs
- **Knee point:** Often-preferred "balanced" solution on front

### 5.2 Metabolic Network Optimization

**Classic application (Sendín et al. 2012, PLoS ONE):**

**Competing objectives:**
1. Maximize metabolic flux (reaction rate, productivity)
2. Minimize energetic cost (enzyme synthesis, regulation)

**Method:** Multi-criteria optimization → Calculate Pareto front

**Finding:** "Knee points" identified as **universal regulation patterns**

### 5.3 Evolutionary Multi-Objective Optimization (EMO)

**Popular algorithms:**
- NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm)
- SPEA-2 (Strength Pareto Evolutionary Algorithm)
- Multi-objective Differential Evolution

**Advantage:** Single run generates approximation of entire Pareto front

### 5.4 Shadow Prices & Adjoint Variables

**Economic interpretation:** How much does optimal value change when relaxing a constraint?

**Biological interpretation:** Which constraints are "expensive" vs. "cheap" to satisfy?

**Application to Δt_Q:** 
- Temporal resolution constraint has high "shadow price" (small changes → large performance impact)
- Metabolic cost constraint has lower shadow price (system can tolerate variation)

### 5.5 Multi-Objective Δt_Q Optimization Model

**Objectives to optimize:**

## 6. GABOR UNCERTAINTY & TIME-FREQUENCY TRADE-OFFS

### 6.1 Fundamental Uncertainty Relation

**Gabor (1946) / Heisenberg Analog:**

$$\Delta t \cdot \Delta f \geq \frac{1}{4\pi}$$

Where:
- Δt = temporal spread (standard deviation)
- Δf = frequency spread (bandwidth)

**Interpretation:** Cannot simultaneously achieve perfect temporal AND frequency resolution.

### 6.2 Daugman's 2D Extension (1985)

**Landmark paper:** *"Uncertainty relation for resolution in space, spatial frequency, and orientation optimized by two-dimensional visual cortical filters"*

**Key results:**
1. **2D Gabor functions** achieve theoretical minimum joint entropy
2. **V1 simple cells** receptive fields match 2D Gabor profiles
3. **Optimal filters** for joint spatial-spectral resolution
4. Receptive fields occupy ~2.5× theoretical minimum area

**Implication:** Visual cortex evolved to **optimize uncertainty relation**.

### 6.3 Information Cells & Quanta

**Gabor's "logon" concept:** 
- Each time-frequency cell contains **one quantum of information**
- Cell size: Δt·Δf = 1 (in natural units)
- Cannot subdivide further without losing information

**Visual analog:**
- Spatial-frequency cells: Δx·Δu ≈ 1/(4π)
- **Cannot improve both** spatial resolution and frequency selectivity

### 6.4 Temporal vs. Spatial Resolution Trade-off

**Jasinschi et al. observation:** For motion processing, optimal filter sizes satisfy:
- **Temporal bandwidth > Spatial bandwidth**
- Constraint to minimize motion uncertainty
- Limited range of allowed filter size combinations

**Connection to Δt_Q:**
- To achieve adequate **spatial resolution** for 3D reconstruction
- Must accept corresponding **temporal integration window**
- Cannot arbitrarily reduce both

### 6.5 Neural Implementation

**Empirical evidence (Daugman 1985, Movshon et al. 1979):**
- V1 simple cells: Gabor-like receptive fields
- Orientation tuning bandwidth: ~30-60°
- Spatial frequency tuning: ~1-2 octaves
- Temporal tuning: ~10-100 Hz range

**Interpretation:** Neural filters **saturate uncertainty bound**.

### 6.6 Calculation for 3D Vision

**Hypothetical derivation:**

Assume required spatial resolution: Δx ≈ 1mm at viewing distance 1m
- Angular resolution: θ ≈ 1 mrad
- Corresponding spatial frequency: f_s ≈ 1000 cycles/m

From uncertainty relation:
- Δx·Δf ≥ 1/(4π)
- If Δx = 1mm = 0.001m, then Δf ≥ 80 cycles/m

For motion with velocity v = 1 m/s:
- Temporal frequency: f_t = f_s·v ≈ 1000 Hz
- Required temporal resolution: Δt ≥ 1/(4π·Δf_t)

**Rough estimate:** To simultaneously resolve spatial detail AND motion:
- Δt ≈ **10-100ms** range

**Observation:** Consistent with neural integration windows!

### 6.7 Predictive Coding & Gabor Filters

**Recent neuroscience (2020s):**
- Temporal uncertainty **enhances** expectation suppression
- Gabor stimuli used to probe visual cortex timing
- Integration windows in MEG: consistent with 100-300ms perception

## 7. MOTION PARALLAX & DEPTH INTEGRATION

### 7.1 Classical View: "Sluggish" Depth Perception

**Historical assumptions (pre-2010):**
- Depth from motion requires long "build-up" time
- Estimates: 600-1000ms (Andersen & Bradley 1998, Treue et al. 1991)
- Based on structure-from-motion (rotating kinetic depth figures)

**Problem:** Long integration times would introduce **depth confusion** as observer moves.

### 7.2 Breakthrough: Brief Integration Times

**Allison, Ono & Brenner (2012) - Journal of Vision:**

**Revolutionary findings:**
1. Relative depth discrimination possible with **16.6ms presentations** (2 frames!)
2. High-contrast pattern mask interrupts processing at SOA **70-75ms**
3. Integration time: **~30ms** (≈2-3 video frames at 60 Hz)

**Method:**
- Random-dot motion parallax stimulus
- Eye-tracker enforced fixation
- Depth-phase discrimination task

### 7.3 Motion/Pursuit Ratio Formula

**Quantitative model (Allison et al.):**

$$\frac{d}{f} \approx \frac{d\theta/dt}{d\alpha/dt}$$

Where:
- d = relative object depth
- f = fixation distance
- dθ/dt = retinal image motion velocity
- dα/dt = pursuit eye movement velocity

**Insight:** Depth extracted from **ratio of two velocity signals**.

### 7.4 Neural Substrate: Area MT

**Middle Temporal (MT) visual area (Nadler et al. 2008, Nature):**

**Key findings:**
1. MT neurons selective for depth from motion parallax
2. **Depth-sign ambiguity** without extra-retinal signals
3. Pursuit/head movement signals **disambiguate** near vs. far
4. Tuning curves shift from symmetric to asymmetric with extra-retinal input

**Interpretation:** MT integrates:
- Retinal image motion (sensory)
- Eye/head movement (motor efference copy)
- → Depth perception

### 7.5 Minimum Frame Requirements

**Empirical evidence:**

| Study | Minimum Duration | Method | Finding |
|-------|------------------|--------|---------|
| **Allison et al. (2012)** | 16.6ms (2 frames) | Depth-phase task | Sufficient for sign |
| **Hosokawa et al. (2013)** | ~30ms | Pattern masking | Integration time |
| **Vergne et al. (2015)** | ~50ms | Motion threshold | Stable performance |

**Critical insight:** While **2 frames minimum** for signal, **30-50ms optimal** for robust perception.

### 7.6 Temporal Recruitment for Sparse Signals

**Structure-from-Motion research (Treue et al., various):**

**Finding:** When each frame shows only 1-2 moving dots (insufficient for 3D):
- **Temporal integration** across 100-200ms enables structure recovery
- Performance improves with longer integration windows
- Optimal dot lifetime: **~150ms**

**Implication:** Sparse signals require **temporal accumulation**.

### 7.7 Translation-Induced Depth Changes

**Critical ecological constraint:**

Consider observer walking at 5 km/h (1.4 m/s):
- In 30ms: translates 4.2 cm
- In 300ms: translates 42 cm
- In 1000ms: translates 1.4 m

**For objects at 1m distance:**
- 30ms translation: depth change <1mm (negligible)
- 300ms translation: depth change ~1cm (detectable but small)
- 1000ms translation: depth change >10cm (significant confusion)

**Conclusion:** Integration window must be:
- **Long enough:** accumulate sufficient parallax signal
- **Short enough:** avoid depth confusion from ongoing motion

### 7.8 Synthesis: Functional Optimum

**Δt_Q ≈ 100-300ms emerges as compromise:**

| Too short (<50ms) | Optimal (100-300ms) | Too long (>500ms) |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Insufficient parallax | Adequate signal | Excess parallax |
| Poor S/N ratio | Optimal S/N | Depth confusion |
| Unstable 3D | Stable 3D perception | Motion lag |
| Flicker perception | Smooth motion | Sluggish response |

**Empirical support:**
- Minimum signal: ~30ms (Allison et al.)
- Sparse signal integration: ~150ms (Treue et al.)
- Neural microstates: 125ms (Lehmann et al.)
- Specious present: 100-300ms (Fraisse, Strawson)

**Protocol:**
- Subjects wear stroboscopic glasses (variable frequency 1-100 Hz)

$$\delta z \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\text{frames}}}} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{f \cdot \Delta t_Q}}$$
