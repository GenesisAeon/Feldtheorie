## Abstract

We present a novel theoretical framework in which spatial and functional dimensions emerge as information-theoretic necessities rather than fundamental features of reality. The central principle—termed the **Frame Principle**—states: *"A dimension emerges when information would otherwise collapse."* This framework unifies three previously disparate theoretical constructs: (1) the Universal Threshold Adaptive Criticality (UTAC) framework with its characteristic β ≈ 4.2 universality, (2) the Regime Integration Gradient (v_RIG) framework describing consciousness as 2D-to-3D information integration at approximately 1.352 km/s, and (3) the Medium-Modulation Hypothesis explaining physical control of consciousness via mechanical soliton waves. We show that each stable dimension requires a protective "1D buffer" or boundary frame, and that consciousness represents the active rendering process by which 2D holographic information becomes 3D volumetric experience. Empirical validation draws from (a) thermodynamic soliton theory of neural transmission (Heimburg-Jackson model), (b) acoustic metamaterial research demonstrating medium-dependent wave propagation, (c) hyperbaric neurophysiology showing pressure-dependent consciousness modulation, and (d) cosmic observations (Böhme et al. 2025) showing velocity alignment with v_RIG predictions (1.3% agreement). We derive six testable predictions including pressure-dependent critical flicker frequency, microtubuli resonance disruption at 13.5 MHz, and emergence thresholds in coupled oscillator networks. This framework suggests consciousness is not epiphenomenal but constitutive—an active dimensional generator necessary for stable three-dimensional structure.

## 2. Theoretical Framework

$$\text{CREP}(d) = \frac{w_{\text{buffer}}(d)}{w_{\text{buffer}}(d) + P_{\text{info}}(d)}$$

**Interpretation:**
- CREP → 0: Buffer collapsing; information loss imminent

### 2.2 The Impedance Modulation Equation

The "stiffness" of the 1D boundary frame is characterized by an effective impedance *Z*_eff which can be modulated by physical medium properties:

$$Z_{\text{eff}} = Z_0 \cdot \gamma(P, T, \chi)$$

where:
- *Z*₀ = α⁻¹ · Φ ≈ 221.74 (fundamental impedance)
- *P* = hydrostatic pressure (modulates frame rigidity)
- *T* = temperature (modulates phase transitions)
- χ = chemical concentration (anesthetics, neuromodulators)

The modulation function γ captures how environmental parameters shift the frame:

$$\gamma(P, T, \chi) = \left(1 + k_P(P - P_0)\right) \cdot \left(1 - k_T(T - T_0)\right) \cdot \left(1 - k_\chi \cdot \chi\right)$$

Typical values: *k*_P ≈ 0.1 atm⁻¹, *k*_T ≈ 0.05 K⁻¹, *k*_χ ≈ 0.2 (normalized concentration).

**Physical Interpretation:**
- **High pressure** (*P* ↑): Frame becomes stiffer → *Z*_eff ↑ → "hyper-integration" (HPNS)
- **High temperature** (*T* ↑): Frame becomes more fluid → *Z*_eff ↓ → approaching under-integration
- **Anesthetics** (χ ↑): Frame dissolves → *Z*_eff ↓ → consciousness loss

This explains the **pressure reversal of anesthesia** [Bennett et al., 1973]: increasing *P* compensates for decreased *Z* due to χ, restoring frame stability.

$$v_{\text{integration}} = v_{\text{RIG}} = \frac{c}{\alpha^{-1} \cdot \Phi}$$

$$\Delta t_P = \frac{\ell_P}{c}$$

$$\Delta t_{\text{integration}} = \frac{\alpha^{-1} \cdot \Phi \cdot \ell_P}{c}$$

The effective integration velocity is then:

$$v_{\text{integration}} = \frac{\text{spatial extent}}{\Delta t_{\text{integration}}} = \frac{c}{\alpha^{-1} \cdot \Phi}$$

**Numerical value:**

$$v_{\text{RIG}} = \frac{299{,}792 \text{ km/s}}{137.036 \times 1.618} \approx 1{,}351.8 \text{ km/s}$$

### 3.1 Thermodynamic Soliton Theory

Heimburg and Jackson [2005, 2007] demonstrated that nerve impulses are not purely electrical but **mechanical density pulses** (solitons) propagating through lipid membranes undergoing phase transitions. Key findings:

1. **Propagation velocity**: ~100-200 m/s (close to sound speed in membranes)
2. **Adiabatic heat**: Reversible temperature changes during action potential
3. **Mechanical displacement**: Axon thickness changes ~0.5 nm during pulse
4. **Phase transition dependence**: Solitons exist only near membrane melting point *T*_m

The wave equation governing soliton propagation:

$$\frac{\partial^2 \Delta\rho}{\partial t^2} = c_0^2 \frac{\partial^2 \Delta\rho}{\partial z^2} + p\Delta\rho\frac{\partial^2 \Delta\rho}{\partial z^2} + q\left(\frac{\partial \Delta\rho}{\partial z}\right)^2 - h\frac{\partial^4 \Delta\rho}{\partial z^4}$$

where Δρ is membrane density change, *c*₀ is linearized sound speed, and *p*, *q*, *h* are nonlinear/dispersive coefficients.

**Critical insight for Frame Principle:**  
The soliton speed depends on medium compressibility:

$$v_{\text{soliton}} = \sqrt{\frac{K_{\text{eff}}}{\rho_{\text{eff}}}}$$

where *K*_eff is effective elastic modulus and ρ_eff is effective density. **This is exactly the 1D buffer width in temporal domain**—how fast information can propagate along the protective frame.

### 3.2 Anesthesia as Frame Dissolution

General anesthetics (ethanol, halothane, isoflurane) work by **lowering the membrane melting temperature** *T*_m [Heimburg & Jackson, 2007]:

$$\Delta T_m = -\frac{R T_m^2}{\Delta H} \cdot x_A$$

where *R* is gas constant, ΔH is melting enthalpy, and *x*_A is mole fraction of anesthetic.

**Mechanism:**  
By shifting *T*_m below physiological temperature (~37°C), the membrane becomes too fluid to support soliton propagation. In Frame Principle terms:

### 3.3 Pressure Reversal: Frame Restoration

High hydrostatic pressure (50-100 atm) reverses anesthetic effects [Johnson & Flagler, 1950; Bennett et al., 1973]. Thermodynamic explanation:

$$\left(\frac{\partial T_m}{\partial P}\right) = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta H}$$
